Computer Architecture — Landmark Papers

A cross-cutting survey of the field's most important papers. Folder: reading-room/computer-architecture/ · canonical references live in the sibling subtopic dirs.

Roughly a dozen papers that define the field, in a sensible reading order (quantitative principles and the ILP/multicore core, then GPUs, warehouse-scale, accelerators, and microarchitectural security). Each entry points to the subtopic dir that treats it in depth.   Legend: PDFopen copy available (see fetch.sh) · DOIpublisher link only.

  1. Validity of the Single Processor Approach to Achieving Large Scale Computing Capabilities — Gene M. Amdahl
    PDFAFIPS 1967 · doi:10.1145/1465482.1465560 · → quantitative-principles/
    The origin of Amdahl's Law and the enduring speedup ceiling imposed by any serial fraction.
  2. An Efficient Algorithm for Exploiting Multiple Arithmetic Units — Robert M. Tomasulo
    DOIIBM J. R&D 1967 · doi:10.1147/rd.111.0025 · → instruction-level-parallelism/
    Register renaming plus the common data bus — the basis of every modern out-of-order engine.
  3. Cache Memories — Alan Jay Smith
    PDFACM Computing Surveys 1982 · doi:10.1145/356887.356892 · → memory-systems-and-storage/
    The definitive early survey establishing the vocabulary and design space of processor caches.
  4. How to Make a Multiprocessor Computer That Correctly Executes Multiprocess Programs — Leslie Lamport
    DOIIEEE TC 1979 · doi:10.1109/TC.1979.1675439 · → multicore-and-memory-consistency/
    The formal definition of sequential consistency — the reference point for every weaker memory model.
  5. Simultaneous Multithreading: Maximizing On-Chip Parallelism — Tullsen, Eggers, Levy
    PDFISCA-22 1995 · doi:10.1145/223982.224449 · → multicore-and-memory-consistency/
    The SMT paper that became Intel Hyper-Threading, filling idle superscalar slots from multiple threads.
  6. The Case for a Single-Chip Multiprocessor — Olukotun, Nayfeh, Hammond, Wilson, Chang
    PDFASPLOS-VII 1996 · doi:10.1145/237090.237140 · → multicore-and-memory-consistency/
    The Hydra argument that many simple cores beat one wide core, presaging the multicore turn.
  7. Roofline: An Insightful Visual Performance Model for Multicore Architectures — Williams, Waterman, Patterson
    DOICACM 2009 · doi:10.1145/1498765.1498785 · → quantitative-principles/
    A single bound-and-bottleneck plot tying attainable performance to operational intensity and bandwidth.
  8. NVIDIA Tesla: A Unified Graphics and Computing Architecture — Lindholm, Nickolls, Oberman, Montrym
    PDFIEEE Micro 2008 · doi:10.1109/MM.2008.31 · → accelerators-and-warehouse-scale/
    The unified-shader SIMT GPU architecture that made GPGPU and later deep learning practical.
  9. The Datacenter as a Computer — Barroso, Hölzle
    PDFSynthesis Lectures 2009 · doi:10.2200/S00193ED1V01Y200905CAC006 · → accelerators-and-warehouse-scale/
    Reframes the warehouse as the unit of computing, founding warehouse-scale architecture and its energy/TCO discipline.
  10. Dark Silicon and the End of Multicore Scaling — Esmaeilzadeh, Blem, St. Amant, Sankaralingam, Burger
    PDFISCA-38 2011 · doi:10.1145/2000064.2000108 · → multicore-and-memory-consistency/
    Models how the end of Dennard scaling caps usable cores, motivating the pivot to specialization.
  11. In-Datacenter Performance Analysis of a Tensor Processing Unit — Jouppi et al.
    PDFISCA-44 2017 · doi:10.1145/3079856.3080246 · → accelerators-and-warehouse-scale/
    Google's deployed systolic-array TPU — the canonical case study in production ML acceleration.
  12. Spectre Attacks: Exploiting Speculative Execution — Kocher et al.
    PDFIEEE S&P 2019 · doi:10.1109/SP.2019.00002 · → tools-and-security/
    Speculation itself becomes a covert channel, opening the transient-execution attack class that reshaped microarchitecture.
